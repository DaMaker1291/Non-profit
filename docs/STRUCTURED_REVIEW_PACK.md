*What this item does not assess:* Marked from the geometry of the line you give, so any two points on the correct line are accepted — the gradient and the intercept are compared with this question's own tolerance, not against a picture. Coordinates are typed; drawing freehand on the grid is not implemented.

**Part a — Give two points on the line y = 2x + 1.**

- Marks: **2** (answer) · response: two points on a line

- Explanation: The gradient is the number multiplying x, so it is 2: from any point on the line, going 1 right means going 2 up. The +1 is the y-intercept — the line crosses the y-axis at (0, 1). (0, 1) and (1, 3) are one valid pair; so are (−1, −1) and (2, 5).

**Part b — Give two points on the line y = −x + 3.**

- Marks: **2** (answer) · response: two points on a line

- Explanation: A gradient of −1 falls as it goes right: 1 right, 1 down. The line crosses the y-axis at (0, 3), so (0, 3) and (2, 1) both lie on it. Writing it as y = 3 − x is the same line; the sign belongs to the x term.

**Part c — Give two points on the line y = 0.5x − 2.**

- Marks: **2** (answer) · response: two points on a line
- Required line: `y = 0.5x + -2` · grid x -4…4, y -5…4 · tolerance m ±0.1, c ±0.3
- Explanation: Half a unit up for every 1 right: that is the gradient 0.5. The line crosses the y-axis at (0, −2), so (0, −2) and (2, −1) lie on it. Choosing a point 2 right is easier here, because the y value then moves a whole unit.

### 4. Fractions and percentages `sf-proportion`

*What this item does not assess:* Each part is worth one mark and is marked from the value you enter. The marker accepts a fraction or an equivalent decimal where the question does not require one particular form.

**Part a — Work out 3/4 of 240.**

- Answer: **180**
- Also accepted: `180`
- Explanation: A quarter of 240 is 60, because 240 ÷ 4 = 60. Three quarters means taking three of those quarter-parts, so 3 × 60 = 180. - Explanation: Fifteen per cent of 240 is 0.15 × 240 = 36. Increasing by 15% means adding that change to the original amount, so 240 + 36 = 276. A single multiplier gives the same answer: 240 × 1.15 = 276, which is often quicker.

Two short exact-value questions. Do not use a decimal approximation.

*What this item does not assess:* Both parts are marked from an exact numeric value. Simplifying a surd is a Higher-tier technique; the marker checks the value, not the spelling of the radical.

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **5**
- Also accepted: `5`
- Explanation: Find a square factor of 50: 50 = 25 × 2, so √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2. The value multiplying √2 is therefore 5, and it is exact. A decimal such as 7.07 approximates the whole surd, not the coefficient k the question asks for.

### 7. Expanding products `hf-algebra`

- **Concept:** `algebra-expand` — Expanding & factorising
- **Tier(s):** Foundation, Higher · **difficulty:** 0.72 · **marks:** 3
- **In the 149-item generated pack:** the concept is, this item is not

Three Higher-tier algebra questions. Enter an expression; equivalent forms are accepted.

*What this item does not assess:* The marker compares the expanded polynomial with the required one. It accepts any equivalent polynomial, including a different order of terms, and it refuses equations or division because they are outside this response type.

**Part a — Expand (x + 3)(x − 2).**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: algebraic expression
- Accepted equivalent forms: `x^2 + x - 6` · expanded form required
- Explanation: Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second: x² − 2x + 3x − 6. Collecting the middle terms gives x² + x − 6. The two middle terms are different signed terms, so they do not vanish.

**Part b — Simplify (2x^3)(3x^2).**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: algebraic expression
- Accepted equivalent forms: `6x^5` · expanded form required
- Explanation: Multiply the coefficients first: 2 × 3 = 6. Then multiply the powers of x by adding their indices: x³ × x² = x^(3+2) = x⁵. The result is 6x⁵, not 6x⁶, because the indices add rather than multiply. The coefficient and the power are separate parts of the term, so they are handled separately before being written together.

**Part c — Expand (2x − 1)^2.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: algebraic expression
- Accepted equivalent forms: `4x^2 - 4x + 1` · expanded form required
- Explanation: Write the square as (2x − 1)(2x − 1), then multiply all four pairs: 4x² − 2x − 2x + 1. The middle terms add to −4x, so the result is 4x² − 4x + 1. It is not 4x² + 1: squaring a bracket does not square its terms separately.

### 10. Angle facts `sf-angle-chain`

- **Concept:** `angles-lines` — Angles & polygons
- **Tier(s):** Foundation, Higher · **difficulty:** 0.48 · **marks:** 5
- **In the 149-item generated pack:** no

Five short angle questions. Each answer is a number of degrees.

*What this item does not assess:* Each part is one mark. The reasons are the standard angle facts; the marker checks the value, and the explanation beside each part states the reason.

**Part a — Angles on a straight line add to how many degrees?**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **180**
- Explanation: A straight angle is half of a full turn. A full turn is 360°, so the angle along a straight line is 360° ÷ 2 = 180°. This is why two adjacent angles on a straight line are called supplementary: together they make a straight angle.

**Part b — A triangle has angles 48° and 65°. Work out the third angle.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **67**
- Also accepted: `67°`
- Explanation: The angles in a triangle add to 180°. The two given angles add to 48 + 65 = 113, so the third is 180 − 113 = 67°. Subtracting from 360 would be the full-turn fact, not the triangle fact.

**Part c — A quadrilateral has angles 90°, 90° and 110°. Work out the fourth angle.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **70**
- Also accepted: `70°`
- Explanation: The four interior angles of a quadrilateral always add to 360°. The three given angles total 90 + 90 + 110 = 290, so the fourth angle must be 360 − 290 = 70°. Subtracting from 180 would apply to a triangle, not a quadrilateral.

**Part d — An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 40°. Work out each base angle.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **70**
- Also accepted: `70°`
- Explanation: The two base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal. Together they share the remaining 180 − 40 = 140°, so each base angle is 140 ÷ 2 = 70°. The 40° apex angle is not one of the equal angles in this triangle.

**Part e — Work out the exterior angle of a regular pentagon.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **72**
- Also accepted: `72°`
- Explanation: The exterior angles of any polygon add to 360°. A regular pentagon has five equal exterior angles, so each one is 360 ÷ 5 = 72°. The interior angle would be 108°, not 72°, because the interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line.

**Part a — An angle is subtended by a diameter at the circumference. Work out its size.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **90**
- Also accepted: `90°`
- Explanation: The angle in a semicircle is a right angle. A diameter cuts the circle into two semicircles, so any angle subtended by that diameter at the circumference is 90°. This is a special case of the angle at the centre being twice the angle at the circumference.

**Part b — Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to how many degrees?**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **180**
- Explanation: A cyclic quadrilateral has all four of its vertices on the same circle. Each pair of opposite angles is supplementary, so the two angles in a pair add to 180°. This is different from the 360° total for all four angles.

**Part c — One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 74°. Work out the opposite angle.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **106**
- Also accepted: `106°`
- Explanation: Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°. The opposite angle is therefore 180 − 74 = 106°. Using 360 here would give the whole quadrilateral rather than the opposite-angle pair, which is the common slip.

**Part d — A tangent meets a radius at the point of contact. Work out the angle between them.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **90**
- Also accepted: `90°`
- Explanation: A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point. At that point of contact, the tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to it, so the angle between the tangent and the radius is 90°.

**Part e — An angle at the centre is 128°. Work out the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.**

- Marks: **1** (answer) · response: numeric entry
- Answer: **64**
- Also accepted: `64°`
- Explanation: When both angles stand on the same arc, the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. The circumference angle is therefore 128 ÷ 2 = 64°, not 128 × 2. The centre angle is the larger of the two.

### 12. A rectangle with an unknown side `hf-rectangle-context`

- **Concept:** `quadratics` — Quadratic equations
- **Tier(s):** Foundation, Higher · **difficulty:** 0.80 · **marks:** 6
- **In the 149-item generated pack:** the concept is, this item is not

A rectangle has length (x + 3) cm and width (x + 2) cm. Its area is 56 cm².

*What this item does not assess:* Part (a) is marked by algebraic equivalence. Part (b) is a numeric solution, and part (c) carries a method mark only when the working is shown. The positive solution is the one that makes a length.

**Part a — Show that the area, in cm², is x² + 5x + 6.**

- Marks: **2** (answer) · response: algebraic expression
- Accepted equivalent forms: `x^2 + 5x + 6` · expanded form required
