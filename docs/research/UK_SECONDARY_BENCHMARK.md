# UK secondary education benchmark

**What this is.** An inspection of publicly reachable UK secondary teaching
resources, focused on how they teach rather than how their homepages look. It
records what was actually visible, what was behind a login or a download, and
what OpenMind should adopt or reject.

**What this is not.** It is not an efficacy trial. A platform's popularity, its
production values or its completion rates do not establish that pupils learned
more. No platform lesson was copied into OpenMind.

**Access note.** The research was done on public pages in November 2026. Oak
National Academy's lesson overview, teacher notes and starter/exit quizzes were
visible, but its slide decks, worksheets and videos could not be inspected
(they rendered as “Loading”). Dr Frost, Sparx and MathsWatch authenticated
question screens were not tested; their published student/teacher guides were
read. Seneca, Cognito, Save My Exams and GCSEPod content was only partly public.
Where a claim depends on a marketing description rather than an observed page,
it says so.

## 1. What the strongest resources actually do

### Oak National Academy — the strongest whole-lesson architecture

Observed pages:

- Maths, Year 8, “Problem solving with linear equations”:
  https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/maths-secondary-ks3/units/solving-linear-equations/lessons/problem-solving-with-linear-equations
- Science, Year 7, “Our solar system”:
  https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/solar-system-and-beyond/lessons/our-solar-system

Oak's teacher-facing page makes the learning outcome, key learning points,
keywords, a named misconception, a teacher tip, a prior-knowledge starter quiz
and an exit quiz visible. The maths unit page shows the 17-lesson sequence and
its prerequisites. Oak describes its intended sequence as: starter quiz →
learning cycles with explanation, checks for understanding and practice →
exit quiz. The actual slide sequence could not be inspected, so this is a
description of the resource model, not a verified account of every lesson.

**Adopt:** make the prerequisite check and the exit check part of the lesson,
not an optional teacher download; name the misconception and the response;
show a concept's place in a sequence.

**Limitation:** the most important teaching artefact — the slide deck — is not
web-readable. A learner cannot inspect the complete lesson without a download
or a teacher. OpenMind should make the whole sequence readable on the page.

### BBC Bitesize — the best free micro-explainer rhythm

Observed page:

- AQA GCSE Biology, cell structure:
  https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/z84jtv4/revision/1

The guide is a sequence of short pages. The first page gives the magnification
formula, a fully worked example (`10 × 4 = 40`), a “Show answer” check and a
figure; the guide has Revise, Video, Audio and Test modes. The pages are short,
one concept at a time, and the exam-board mapping is explicit.

**Adopt:** short pages, a worked example next to the formula, reveal-answer
checks, and a visible route from explanation to test.

**Limitation:** it is mostly tell-and-test. Misconceptions are not systematically
diagnosed. The observed cell-structure page also contained a wrong link to an
energy-stores topic — an editorial quality-control failure that a static
content check would not catch.

### Seneca — strongest assessment taxonomy, fragmented teaching

Observed topic map:

- AQA GCSE Biology, animal cells:
  https://senecalearning.com/en-GB/revision-notes/gcse/biology/aqa/higher/1-1-10-animal-cells

The topic map distinguishes content, calculation, required-practical,
exam-style, diagnostic-misconception and end-of-topic-test nodes. That taxonomy
is better than a single “quiz” bucket: it recognises that a calculation, a
practical and a misconception probe are different kinds of evidence. The free
tier is broad; extended practice and AI-marked questions are paid. The public
marketing pages gave inconsistent counts of open-ended questions, which is a
transparency warning rather than a teaching finding.

**Adopt:** the assessment taxonomy. **Reject:** fragmenting a topic so finely
that the learner never meets a connected explanation.

### Cognito — the strongest free video-to-exam bridge

Observed GCSE Physics pages:

- https://go.cognitoedu.org/physics
- https://cognito.org/specifications/gcse/physics/aqa

Cognito combines short animated video, notes, key terms, quizzes, flashcards,
exam questions and mark schemes, mapped to exam boards. The public pages
establish the mix; they do not establish that a particular lesson's feedback
diagnoses reasoning rather than marking an answer. Some assets have free-tier
caps.

**Adopt:** connect the explanation to a real exam-style question and its mark
scheme. **Do not** treat a video view as evidence of learning.

### Dr Frost Maths — the clearest joined-up maths practice loop

Observed published guides:

- Student guide: https://www.drfrost.org/docs/DFM-GettingStartedForStudents.pdf
- Teacher guide: https://www.drfrost.org/docs/DrFrost-GettingStartedForTeachers.pdf

The student guide documents: a preview example, a short worked-example video,
randomly generated Key Skills practice, exam practice, algebraic answer entry
that can accept equivalent forms, a response explanation whether right or
wrong, difficulty responding to correctness, and a configurable accuracy target.
The teacher guide documents assignment by topic or question and item-level
performance views.

**Adopt:** a worked example immediately before practice; equivalent-form answer
entry; feedback that explains; a fresh retry after an error; teacher visibility
of item-level errors.

**Limitation:** the guides are descriptions, not a live test of today's
interface. The strongest missing evidence is whether the explanations diagnose
the learner's actual misconception rather than merely restating the method.

### Sparx Maths — strongest retrieval/homework system

Observed support pages:

- Student support: https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/345064-helping-your-students-to-make-the-most-of-sparx-maths
- Personalisation: https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342284-how-does-sparx-personalise-homework
- Bookwork checks: https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342329-understanding-bookwork-checks
- Homework insights: https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342340-using-homework-insights

Sparx documents a short support video per question, near-miss hints, a changed
question after repeated failure, personalised homework and consolidation from
topics roughly three and ten weeks earlier. Bookwork checks ask a pupil to recall
a recent answer without showing the question. Teachers see completion, struggle
and class-level error patterns.

**Adopt:** spaced cumulative retrieval; a changed question after failure; a
distinction between supported practice and a formal test; item-level teacher
insight. **Limitation:** a “similar” worked example is not necessarily an
explanation of the pupil's exact error, and the number of variants is finite.

### MathsWatch — the strongest formative/summative distinction

Observed guides:

- Student/parent guide: https://vle.mathswatch.co.uk/downloads/kf0xc76y/mathswatch_student_guide.pdf
- Teacher instructions: https://vle.mathswatch.co.uk/downloads/vle_instructions_for_teachers.pdf

MathsWatch documents full-length and one-minute videos, interactive questions,
homework that allows help videos and repeat attempts until a deadline, and tests
that are timed, one-attempt, hide videos and auto-submit. It also documents
marking some questions for working, not only the final answer.

**Adopt:** do not use one mode for both learning and assessment. A supported
practice attempt and an independent test are different evidence. **Caveat:** the
authenticated interface and exact algebraic marking were not tested.

### Corbettmaths and Maths Genie — open practice and exam transfer

Observed pages:

- Corbettmaths contents: https://corbettmaths.com/contents/
- Corbettmaths 5-a-day: https://corbettmaths.com/5-a-day/gcse
- Maths Genie GCSE: https://mathsgenie.co.uk/gcse/maths
- Maths Genie question bank: https://mathsgenie.co.uk/gcse/maths/edexcel/questions

Both provide topic video, practice and full solutions, often organised by grade.
Corbettmaths' 5-a-day is a simple, effective mixed-retrieval routine. The public
pages do not establish adaptive sequencing or diagnostic feedback.

**Adopt:** a daily mixed retrieval routine; grade/orientation labels on practice;
full solutions that a learner can check against their own working.

### Isaac Physics / Isaac Science — problem-solving, not recall

Observed pages:

- About: https://isaacscience.org/about
- Question finder: https://isaacphysics.org/questions
- Solving problems: https://isaacscience.org/solving_problems
- Student FAQs: https://isaacscience.org/support/student
- Mentoring: https://isaacscience.org/pages/mentoring

Isaac separates practice questions (typically one concept or equation) from
challenge questions (multiple concepts or a more creative application). Its
problem-solving guidance asks pupils to identify the goal and the relevant
information, use clues in the wording to choose assumptions, and decide whether
a force or energy approach is appropriate. Mentoring asks pupils to attempt
challenging problems before discussion, which protects the thinking that a
demonstration can otherwise replace.

**Adopt:** a visible problem-solving routine (goal → givens → representation →
model/assumption → solve → unit/plausibility check); practice and challenge
tiers; an independent attempt before a solution is revealed.

### Science resources: RSC, STEM Learning, Save My Exams, PMT, FreeScienceLessons

Observed pages:

- RSC calculations lesson: https://edu.rsc.org/lesson-plans/practising-calculations-in-chemistry-14-16-years/96.article
- RSC representations activity: https://edu.rsc.org/atoms-and-molecules/elements-compounds-and-mixtures-developing-understanding-11-14-years/4023933.article
- RSC maths skills: https://edu.rsc.org/resources/collections/maths-skills-for-chemistry
- STEM physics activities: https://www.stem.org.uk/resources/library/resource/32774/nuffield-physics-activities-for-gcse
- Save My Exams practical guide: https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/exam-guides/how-to-answer-required-practical-method-questions-in-aqa-gcse-science
- PMT past papers: https://www.physicsandmathstutor.com/past-papers/gcse-science/
- FreeScienceLessons: https://www.freesciencelessons.co.uk/gcse-physics-paper-2/waves/

RSC's calculation lesson models a calculation, uses paired checkpoint questions,
then asks pupils to complete final calculations independently. Its
representations activity deliberately links visible, particle-level and symbolic
representations, with questions getting harder. STEM's practical collections
emphasise enquiry, evidence and interpretation rather than recipe-following.
Save My Exams and PMT are strongest at exam-style questions and mark schemes;
they are not a complete taught sequence. FreeScienceLessons is a topic/video
index, with questions and progress tracking in a separate paid tier.

**Adopt:** coordinated representations; a worked calculation followed by paired
checks and independent completion; practical evidence questions; mark-scheme
literacy. **Do not** mistake a question bank for a lesson.

### GCSEPod, Educake and Quizlet

- GCSEPod is school-subscription only; public marketing describes Pods and
  Check & Challenge, but the actual Pods and challenge flow require a school
  login. Public evidence is insufficient to judge its hint ladder. Access:
  https://www.gcsepod.com/student-resources
- Educake is a paid retrieval/homework engine, not a lesson sequence; public
  sample quizzes are limited. Access/pricing:
  https://www.educake.co.uk/subscribe
- Quizlet is a learner-owned flashcard layer. Learn/Test modes are limited on the
  free tier and teacher-managed classes unlock more. Access:
  https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030841732-Studying-on-Quizlet

**Use:** retrieval scheduling and low-stakes recall. **Do not** treat flashcard
recognition as conceptual understanding.

### Digestion sequence check — Oak and BBC (batch 15)

Observed pages:

- Oak, KS3 human digestive system unit:
  https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/human-digestive-system/lessons
- Oak, “Digestion and enzymes”:
  https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/science-secondary-ks3/units/human-digestive-system/lessons/digestion-and-enzymes
- BBC Bitesize, KS3 structure of the digestive system:
  https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/articles/zv8m7yc
- BBC Bitesize, AQA GCSE human digestive system:
  https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zcttv9q/revision/4

Oak's unit page presents five lessons in sequence: the parts of the digestive
system, digestion and enzymes, food tests, bacteria in the digestive system,
and absorption and transport of nutrients. The “Digestion and enzymes” page
states the outcome as describing mechanical and chemical digestion and the role
of enzymes, and it exposes a six-question prior-knowledge starter and a
six-question exit quiz. The visible quiz items distinguish mechanical from
chemical digestion, match organs and glands to their functions, and test whether
digestion turns soluble food into insoluble food. Oak's teacher tip uses a
paperchain model; the slide deck, worksheet and video rendered as “Loading”, so
only the page structure and visible quiz items were inspected.

BBC's KS3 page separates mechanical from chemical digestion, describes the
mouth, oesophagus, stomach, liver and pancreas, and explains bile as
emulsification that increases surface area rather than as digestion itself. Its
GCSE page describes the ileum's folds, villi and microvilli, a thin wall and a
blood supply as absorption adaptations. These are visible page descriptions,
not an efficacy claim.

**Adopted:** the sequence from organs to mechanical/chemical digestion to
absorption; the explicit separation of bile from enzymes; the surface-area
explanation of emulsification; and the villus as a structure with a transport
system. **Not copied:** Oak's paperchain activity, quiz wording, diagrams or
video, and BBC's text. The OpenMind lesson is independently written and uses a
numerical surface-area example and a blocked-bile-duct case that were not taken
from either page.

## 2. The recurring design pattern

The strongest resources are not distinguished by video quality. They combine:

1. **A prerequisite check** that can change what happens next.
2. **A short, concrete introduction** before terminology.
3. **A worked example that names the reason for each move.**
4. **A faded attempt** — completion or first-step-only — before independence.
5. **Practice that gives item-level feedback**, not only a score.
6. **A misconception probe** with a contrast, not a label.
7. **A transfer task** in an unfamiliar context.
8. **Retrieval after a delay**, not only at the end of the lesson.
9. **A visible distinction between supported practice and independent test.**
10. **Teacher-visible error patterns**, because a class's wrong answers are
    teaching information.

The market's clearest gap is **worked reasoning and extended-answer feedback**.
Most platforms explain or quiz; few model expert thinking and then coach a
learner through the point where their own reasoning diverges. OpenMind can
differentiate there, but only if it authors the reasoning rather than generating
another paragraph.

## 3. What OpenMind has, and what it lacks

OpenMind's existing authored-lesson contract already contains the right eleven
parts: objectives, prior knowledge, introduction, explanation, representations,
worked examples, misconceptions, guided practice, assessment, transfer and
retrieval. Its subject frameworks refuse a physics answer without a unit, a
chemistry equation without balanced atoms, a biology structure without a
function, a computing lesson without code, trace and test, and a maths example
that jumps to the answer.

The audit published beside this report shows the gap:

- **16 of 135** concepts have an authored lesson meeting that contract.
- **119** are plan outlines; many are assembled from a genome paragraph and a
  concept lens.
- **0** have subject-teacher review.
- **0** have learner-testing evidence for transfer or delayed retention.

The next content work must therefore be authoring, not another interface layer.
