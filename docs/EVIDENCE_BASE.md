# The evidence base a deployment would stand on

**Status: DRAFT. Compiled October 2026 from public sources. Nothing here has been
reviewed by a subject specialist or a lawyer, and no figure marked `provisional`
should be quoted in a funding proposal without someone opening the source page.**
Every row names its scope and its status; the point of the table is that a reader
can see the difference between a number somebody read and a number a search
engine reported.

---

## How to read this

Each figure carries one of three statuses, and they are not interchangeable:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `checked` | The researcher opened the source page and read the figure there. The page is linked. |
| `provisional` | The figure came from a search index, summary or secondary mention and the source page could not be fetched. It may be right; nobody has confirmed it. |
| `not found` | No usable figure was located. This is recorded rather than filled with an adjacent number. |

A `provisional` figure is a lead, not evidence. If this document is used to argue
for money, the provisional rows must be either verified or deleted — a ministry
officer who checks one and finds it wrong will discount the rest.

Two further rules throughout:

- **Scope is part of the fact.** A global figure is not a low-income-country
  figure. "Share of schools with computers" is not a pupil-per-device ratio.
  "Registered users" is not "learners reached", and neither is learning.
- **A report's publication year is not its data year.** Where the data year could
  not be established, the row says so.

### The Mexico trial

The strongest impact evidence located is a randomised controlled trial in Mexico
City and the State of Mexico: **45 public upper-secondary schools, nearly 3,000
students, mathematics**, curriculum-aligned remedial content with teacher
training, statistically significant learning gains, stronger among consistent
users. Published January 2026 by [UNICEF Innocenti](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/impact-digital-learning-mexico).

Two caveats that belong beside it whenever it is cited:

1. **It was published by UNICEF's own research office**, not an independent
   evaluator. That does not make it wrong; it means "an independent RCT" is the
   wrong description.
2. It establishes an effect **for one implementation, one subject, one country**.
   The study itself names teacher engagement, school leadership, infrastructure
   and equity measures as conditions for effectiveness — which is the same
   lesson as section 6.

### What this means for positioning

The programme has reach, a convening partner, a published platform, and now a
trial. A new entrant does not beat that on scale, and should stop describing the
relationship as a contest. The defensible differences are structural: whether
the deployment can run without a foreign cloud, whether a country can take its
data and leave, whether the content licence permits local modification, and
whether the total cost is printable.

**Timelines, as observed rather than averaged:** Tulna took roughly two years
from development to documented use in three states' procurement; Learning
Passport was announced in 2018, piloted in 2020 under pandemic pressure, and
reported 47 countries by 2025. A planning judgment (not a measured average) is
one to two years to establish fit, permissions and a meaningful pilot, and
several more for evaluation, budget line, localisation and expansion.

**Failure modes the sources name**, in the order they actually kill deployments:
hardware without an instructional problem; a product that does not fit
curriculum, language or timetable; one-off training with no coaching budget; a
pilot without a named ministry counterpart or recurrent-budget pathway;
registrations counted as learning; total cost of ownership underestimated; data
protection and safeguarding treated as post-launch work; and vendor lock-in that
makes a ministry hesitate before it has signed.

Nearer to this project: [PILOT_SCOPE.md](PILOT_SCOPE.md) records the pilot design
and the three things it still lacks — a review workflow, a consent flow, and an
outcome instrument independent of the product's own questions.

**The compliance floor is a planning estimate, not a published benchmark:** for a
two-to-five-person organisation using local partners in one pilot country, budget
roughly **$8,000–25,000 a year** for insurance, filings, privacy operations, one
country's registration and a modest safeguarding programme — before staff,
software development or programme delivery. Certification, multiple country
registrations, an audit or public tenders take it well above that.

## 8. What this does not establish

- **No independent impact evidence for this product.** Nothing here is an
  evaluation of OpenMind. The 110 unreviewed pilot items and the single-class
  pilot design in [PILOT_SCOPE.md](PILOT_SCOPE.md) are the beginning of one.
- **No verified income-group breakdown of school infrastructure.** The four rows
  in section 1 are provisional and global in scope.
- **No cost-of-deployment figure.** Nothing here prices devices, connectivity,
  teacher training or support in any country. The only costs stated are the
  organisation's own compliance floor (an estimate) and US insurance medians.
- **No comparison with Learning Passport at the level a ministry would ask for.**
  Its per-country total cost, procurement terms and software licence are not
  public; a cost comparison would be speculation.
- **No legal conclusion.** Section 6 is a framework for questions to put to
  counsel in each operating country, not an answer to any of them.
- **No claim that any figure here would survive a ministry's own verification.**
  The `provisional` rows exist so that this document says which ones it might not.
