# Country comparison

> This is a **design comparison**, not a league table. The figures below come from
> different years, instruments and populations; they are not directly comparable
> as measures of one education system against another. Each figure is repeated
> from a country profile where its source, method and limitations are recorded.

## What the comparison actually shows

1. **The binding constraint is not the same everywhere.** Malawi's binding
   constraint is material scarcity and class size. Rwanda's is the English medium
   versus Kinyarwanda comprehension. India's is shared-device access and board
   diversity. The Philippines' is language-policy change plus disaster continuity.
2. **A national average is not a school.** Nigeria's regional reading range and
   Pakistan's provincial out-of-school range are the clearest examples. The
   product must ask for the actual context.
3. **Foundational learning is a secondary-school problem too.** Bangladesh, Ghana,
   Kenya, Nepal, Tanzania and Zambia all show large shares below foundational
   benchmarks. A Grade 8/Form 1/JSS 1 label cannot be a placement rule.
4. **Language is not one setting.** The learner may need one language to
   understand, another for the subject term, and another for the examination. The
   existing language profile already separates teaching, answer, school and exam
   language; the content coverage does not yet match that model.
5. **Connectivity statistics are not classroom readiness.** Bangladesh's
   institution-level electricity/internet figures and Indonesia's PISA resource
   reports do not prove that a given lesson can stream video or assume a device
   per pupil.

## What is not comparable

- Bangladesh's 51% learning poverty, Kenya's 79%, the Philippines' 91% and
  Zambia's 98.5% use learning-poverty estimates from different underlying years
  and instruments. They are not a ranking.
- PISA 2025 describes participating 15-year-olds, not all children in Indonesia.
- Nigeria's regional reading estimates come from different collection dates; the
  UNICEF report itself warns against treating them as one examination.
- Ghana's "over 60% fail basic literacy and numeracy" is not the same instrument
  as ASER's Std III reading task or Tanzania's Standard 2 benchmark.
- A national PTR is not a class size. Bangladesh's 29:1 national ratio and its
  reported 40–70 state-primary classes are both true and measure different things.
