# Indonesia (ID)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Indonesia's PISA results describe
> participating 15-year-olds, not every child and not every island. This profile
> does not establish a reviewed Kurikulum Merdeka mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Indonesia · SMP/SMA**, SMP 7–9 and SMA 10–12, under
Kurikulum Merdeka. Vocational (SMK), madrasah, religious and non-formal routes are
not fully modelled. Provincial and island differences are not represented.

## 2. Curriculum

Kurikulum Merdeka and the SMP/SMA mathematics/science syllabi have not been
reviewed. The registry's mapping remains `mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

The registry records US SMP and US SMA. Current national assessment, school
examinations and practical requirements have not been reviewed.

PISA 2025's 17% mathematics Level 2 is far below the OECD average of 65%; it is a
serious signal, but it does not describe primary schools, rural islands or
children who have left school.

## 5. Foundational skills

The PISA results show that most participating 15-year-olds do not reach Level 2
mathematics. A Grade 7 learner may still need foundational arithmetic and
reading. The product must not equate the SMP year with mastery.

## 6. Teacher conditions

No current national teacher-ratio or qualification figure has been extracted.
Teacher deployment across provinces and islands remains unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

The PISA principal reports of material and infrastructure shortages support a
low-resource design, but they are perceptions from participating schools. The
product must support large classes, shared materials and no-device teaching.

## 8. Textbook availability

No current national pupil-textbook ratio has been verified. The PISA material
shortage signal is not a textbook ratio. The lesson must not require a textbook in
every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

Bahasa Indonesia is the national language and a major medium of instruction;
regional languages are used in communities and some education provision. The
current language policy and classroom practice in a given province have not been
reviewed.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Bahasa Indonesia and regional languages such as Javanese, Sundanese, Balinese and
many others are relevant. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. The lesson
must work offline and on paper. PISA's material-shortage signal does not establish
connectivity.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power or a projector.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

No current gender-disaggregated PISA figure has been extracted. Disability,
language, rural/island, poverty and out-of-school barriers remain unverified.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

Indonesia's provincial and island geography is not represented by a national PISA
sample. A resource suitable for a Jakarta school is not automatically suitable for
a remote island school. No current rural/urban comparison has been reviewed.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Structured pedagogy, foundational catch-up and low-resource teacher support are
relevant. This pass did not review an Indonesian trial of OpenMind or a specific
intervention's effect size.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Do not treat PISA's participating students as all Indonesian children.**
2. **Support Bahasa Indonesia and regional-language explanation** without claiming
   reviewed translation.
3. **Use teacher-led no-device and limited-materials modes.**
4. **Place by prerequisite**, not by SMP/SMA year.
5. **Localise examples and units** for the province and community.
6. **Do not claim Kurikulum Merdeka alignment.**
