# Kenya (KE)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** This profile records a strong
> learning-poverty signal and the language/curriculum questions that follow from
> it. It does not establish a reviewed KCSE or CBC mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Kenya · Secondary**, Forms 1–4, ending in KCSE under KNEC.
The current Kenyan competency-based curriculum (CBC) phase structure and whether
this registry record matches it have **not** been verified in this pass. The
route may therefore be structurally stale.

This is a structural warning, not a claim that the route is wrong: the registry
must be checked against the current system before a Kenyan learner is placed.

## 2. Curriculum

The CBC and the current secondary subject syllabi have not been reviewed. The
registry's concept mapping remains `mapped`. No claim is made that OpenMind's
concepts match the Kenyan sequence or its competency expectations.

## 3. Examinations

The registry records KCPE and KCSE. KCPE has been replaced in the CBC transition;
the current national assessment arrangements, KCSE papers and practical
requirements have not been reviewed. The route may be stale and is flagged as
such.

tell a Kenyan child they are behind.

## 5. Foundational skills

The 79% learning-poverty figure means that reading comprehension is a system-wide
constraint. A learner may read an English question without understanding the
mathematical or scientific concept behind it. Assessment must separate reading
demand from subject understanding where possible.

## 6. Teacher conditions

No current national teacher-ratio, qualification or deployment figure has been
extracted for this profile. Teacher training and classroom conditions remain
unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

No current national class-size observation has been reviewed. The product must
support large classes, shared materials and no-device teaching rather than assume
a well-resourced laboratory or a device per pupil.

## 8. Textbook availability

No current national pupil-textbook ratio has been verified. The lesson must not
require a textbook in every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

English and Kiswahili are the official languages; English is the language of
national examinations and a major medium of instruction, while Kiswahili is widely
used and taught. The CBC's language policy and local-language practice in a given
school have not been reviewed.

## 10. Common teaching languages

English and Kiswahili are the main school languages; many pupils also speak a
community language. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for. Reading an
English question is not proof of understanding the concept.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. Household
mobile access is not school connectivity, and a school computer is not a personal
device.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power; a charged device and printed pack are the baseline.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

No current gender-disaggregated learning figure has been extracted. Disability,
language, rural/urban, poverty and displacement barriers remain unverified and
must not be assumed away by a national average.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

No county-level comparison has been reviewed. The national learning-poverty
figure does not describe a particular county, school or classroom.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Foundational literacy and numeracy support, structured pedagogy and teacher-led
large-class routines are relevant. This pass did not review a Kenyan trial of
OpenMind or a specific intervention's effect size.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Reconcile the route with the CBC before claiming alignment.** The Form 1–4
   registry record may not match the current system.
2. **Do not equate English reading with subject understanding.**
3. **Place by prerequisite and offer foundational reading/numeracy support.**
4. **Use teacher-led no-device and shared-device modes.**
5. **Keep English/Kiswahili terminology explicit** without claiming reviewed
   translation.
6. **Do not claim KCSE/CBC mapping.**

## 21. Unverified questions

- What is the current CBC phase structure and which years does the registry's
  Form 1–4 route actually correspond to?
- What are the current national assessment and KCSE requirements?
- What are the language-of-instruction rules and classroom practices?
- What are actual class sizes, textbook ratios and school resources?
- How do county, rural/urban and household-income differences affect learning?
- Which learners are out of school, over-age or returning after interruption?
- What disability and language barriers exist in the classroom?
