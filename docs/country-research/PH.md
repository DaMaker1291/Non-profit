# Philippines (PH)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** The Philippines is a case where the
> language policy changed recently. This profile records the current policy and
> the learning-poverty signal; it does not establish a reviewed MATATAG mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Philippines · JHS/SHS**, Grades 7–12, under DepEd. The
current MATATAG curriculum, the Alternative Learning System (ALS), madrasah
education and technical-vocational routes are not fully modelled. Disaster
interruption is a recurring feature of schooling and is not modelled at all.

## 2. Curriculum

The MATATAG curriculum and the Grade 7–12 mathematics/science syllabi have not
been reviewed. The registry's mapping remains `mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

## 5. Foundational skills

The learning-poverty estimate means that reading comprehension is a system-wide
constraint. A Grade 7 learner may read a question without understanding the
science or mathematics behind it. Assessment should separate reading demand from
subject understanding where possible.

## 9. Language policy

Republic Act 12027 and DepEd Order 020 (2025) changed the earlier mother-tongue
policy. From School Year 2025–2026, Filipino and English are the primary media of
instruction in Kindergarten to Grade 3, with Filipino Sign Language for deaf
learners. Mother-tongue instruction is optional in monolingual classes where
specific readiness criteria are met; regional and local languages may be used as
auxiliary media, including translanguaging and bridging.

This profile uses the 2025 policy, not older descriptions of mandatory
mother-tongue instruction.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Filipino and English are the primary school languages under the current policy;
regional languages may be used as auxiliary media. A pupil's strongest language
must be asked for, and the examination language must be kept visible.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

No current rural/urban comparison has been reviewed. The Philippines' geography
and disaster exposure mean that a lesson may need to survive school closure,
displacement or intermittent connectivity.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later. A downloaded pack should remain usable after a disaster interrupts
the school day.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Structured pedagogy, foundational catch-up, bilingual/translanguaging support and
disaster-resilient delivery are relevant. This pass did not review a Philippine
trial of OpenMind or a specific intervention's effect size.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Use the 2025 language policy**, not the older mandatory mother-tongue rule.
2. **Support Filipino/English with regional-language auxiliary explanation.**
3. **Recognize Filipino Sign Language as an accessibility requirement**, not a
   translation afterthought.
4. **Support ALS and disaster-interrupted learners** with portable, offline,
   printable packs.
5. **Place by prerequisite**, not by Grade 7–12 label.
6. **Do not claim MATATAG alignment.**
