# Ghana (GH)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Ghana is a teacher-support case:
> language transition, uneven deployment and shared textbooks matter more than a
> device-per-pupil story. This profile does not establish a reviewed NaCCA,
> BECE or WASSCE mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Ghana · JHS/SHS**, JHS 1–3 and SHS 1–3, with BECE and
WASSCE under the Ghana Education Service. Primary, kindergarten, technical and
vocational, and non-formal routes are not fully modelled.

## 2. Curriculum

The standards-based curriculum and the JHS/SHS mathematics and science syllabi
have not been reviewed. The registry's mapping remains `mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

BECE and WASSCE are recorded. Their formats, practical papers, school-based
assessment and subject combinations have not been reviewed.

## 5. Foundational skills

The gap between 11.6 years of schooling and 5.7 effective learning years is a
concise statement of the problem: attendance is not learning. A JHS 1 learner may
still need foundational reading and numeracy.

## 6. Teacher conditions

The national primary pupil-teacher ratio is 32:1, but over 14% of schools exceed
50:1, and rural/northern districts face higher class sizes. The 2025 brief also
reports undertrained teachers and inadequate infrastructure. Teacher subject
knowledge and in-service support remain unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

The evidence supports large-class and uneven-deployment modes, not a uniform
Ghanaian classroom. No current national distribution of multigrade or double-shift
classes has been reviewed.

## 8. Textbook availability

UNICEF Innocenti reports that the average student has access to about half of
their required subject textbooks. That is a national average, not a per-school
guarantee. The lesson must not require a textbook in every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

Ghana has over 80 languages. The language-in-education policy uses one of 12
government-approved Ghanaian languages as the medium for Kindergarten 1 to
Primary 3, with English as the main medium from Primary 4 onwards. English is a
subject in lower primary; Ghanaian languages are taught as subjects later.

Implementation is uneven: teacher deployment may not match the approved language,
and the policy has been described as unclear in parts. The exact practice in a
given school has not been verified.

## 10. Common teaching languages

English is the official language and the later medium. Akuapem Twi, Asante Twi,
Fante, Nzema, Dagaare, Dagbanli, Ewe, Dangme, Ga, Gonja and Kasem are among the
approved instructional languages. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. The lesson
must work offline and on paper.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power or a projector.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

The 2025 brief reports rural and disadvantaged areas as the main concern but does
not give a gender-disaggregated learning series in the excerpt reviewed. Gender,
disability, language and poverty barriers remain unverified.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

Rural and northern districts face higher class sizes and teacher shortages. That
is a directional signal, not a full rural/urban comparison. A national average
must not be presented as the typical rural school.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Teacher deployment, structured pedagogy, language-matched teaching and
foundational catch-up are relevant. This pass did not review a Ghanaian trial of
OpenMind or a specific intervention's effect size. Teaching at the Right Level
evidence from Ghana is noted in the wider literature but is not treated here as
proof for OpenMind.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Support the Ghanaian-language-to-English transition** with bilingual
   explanation and consistent subject terminology.
2. **Make teacher lesson preparation and large-class routines central.**
3. **Assume shared textbooks and no laboratory.**
4. **Place by prerequisite**, not by JHS/SHS year.
5. **Do not claim NaCCA/BECE/WASSCE alignment.**
6. **Do not infer a pupil's language from the school's approved language.**
