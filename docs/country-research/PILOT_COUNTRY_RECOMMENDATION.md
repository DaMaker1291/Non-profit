# Pilot country recommendation

> This is a research recommendation, not a launch plan. No school, teacher,
> learner or education authority has been contacted. Any learner pilot requires
> school approval, safeguarding, adult supervision and appropriate consent.

## Recommendation

Start with **three deliberately different systems**:

| Pilot | What it tests | Why this system, from the evidence | What must be true before contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bangladesh** | Bengali-first teaching, foundational placement, large primary/secondary classes, print/no-device | 51% learning poverty; about half of Grade 3/5 pupils below grade-level Bangla; state primary classes of 40–70 reported; high institution-level electricity but no pupil-device guarantee | A reviewed Bangla teaching sample; a subject-specialist review; a school partner and safeguarding approval |
| **Malawi** | Extreme material scarcity, 90+ pupil classes, shared textbooks, print-first, no laboratory | 19% reading / 13% numeracy; PTR 61; 86% of schools worse than 3:1 textbooks; 66% of lower-primary schools above 90 pupils per permanent classroom | A print pack that actually works without a textbook; a teacher run sheet tested for preparation time; a school partner |
| **Nigeria** | Multilingual classes, regional/state differences, non-formal learners, large classes, offline | 27% reading / 25% numeracy; 10.2m + 8.1m out of school; North West 9.5% vs South East 56%; PTR 47 | A state/board/language decision; a reviewed language sample for the chosen state; a partner that can reach the intended learners |

The synthetic delivery scenarios for these and five further systems are in
[`SCHOOL_ARCHETYPES.md`](SCHOOL_ARCHETYPES.md); they are design cases, not
representative schools.

These three do **not** represent every country or every school within those
countries. They are chosen because they stress different parts of the system:
language and foundational learning (Bangladesh), material scarcity and class size
(Malawi), and linguistic/regional complexity (Nigeria).

## Why not start with all 70

A 70-country launch would produce 70 shallow profiles and no evidence that any
child learned more. The binding constraint is not content volume; it is whether a
teacher can use the material in a real classroom and whether a learner can
demonstrate independent understanding afterwards.

## What a pilot must measure

Do not measure lessons opened, videos watched, questions generated or accounts
created. Measure:

1. **Baseline understanding** of the target concept and its prerequisites.
2. **Independent post-teaching performance** on a parallel form, without hints.
3. **Transfer** to an unfamiliar question.
4. **Delayed retention** after a interval agreed with the school.
5. **Misconception resolution**, not just a changed answer.
6. **Teacher usability**: preparation time, clarity, workload, curriculum fit,
   resource requirements.
7. **Language comprehension**: whether pupils understood the explanation and the
   examination term.
8. **Equity**: who was excluded by device, language, disability, gender or cost.

A group answer, a teacher-reported impression or a correct click after a worked
example is not individual mastery evidence.

## What each pilot would need to falsify

- **Bangladesh:** Can a teacher with one computer and a large class teach the
  lesson without a Bengali translation that has not been reviewed? If not, the
  language work is the blocker, not the print path.
- **Malawi:** Can a teacher with 90+ pupils and shared textbooks run the lesson
  from a printed pack in the available time? If not, the teacher pack is too long
  or the group routine is unrealistic.
- **Nigeria:** Can the same lesson work in two different language/state contexts
  without pretending they are one system? If not, the route model is too coarse.

## Expansion order after the first three

Kenya, Nepal, Tanzania, Ghana, Zambia, Rwanda, Pakistan, India and the
Philippines. The order is based on distinct design cases, not on which country
has the most flattering statistics. Each expansion needs its own language,
curriculum and school-partner review.

## Current blockers

- **0 reviewed curriculum mappings**, **0 reviewed teaching translations**, **0
  teacher reviewers** and **0 learner-tested lessons**.
- No school partner, ethics/safeguarding approval or consent process.
- No verified school-level electricity, connectivity or device data for most
  routes.
- No physical printing, real carrier, low-cost hardware or school-LAN test.
- The Kenya route may be structurally stale after the CBC transition.

## What would count as success

Not "70 country routes". Success would be evidence that, in at least one
low-resource classroom per pilot, pupils who were below the expected level could
explain a concept, solve an unfamiliar problem and retain it later — and that
their teacher could prepare and run the lesson without a personal device for every
child or a permanent internet connection.
