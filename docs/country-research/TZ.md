# Tanzania (TZ)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Tanzania's language transition is the
> clearest design case in this cohort. This profile covers mainland Tanzania;
> Zanzibar has separate arrangements that are not yet researched here.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Tanzania · Secondary**, Forms 1–4, ending in CSEE under
NECTA, with PSLE. The registry does not distinguish mainland and Zanzibar
systems, or model technical, vocational and non-formal routes. The route is a
mainland secondary record, not a complete national system.

## 2. Curriculum

The secondary mathematics and science syllabi, the 3Rs primary reform and the
current subject sequence have not been reviewed. The registry's mapping remains
`mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

PSLE and CSEE are recorded. The current examination formats, practical work,
school-based assessment and Zanzibar arrangements have not been reviewed.

## 5. Foundational skills

The Standard 2 results show that foundational reading and arithmetic are weak
before secondary school. A Form 1 learner may be below the primary benchmark, and
the language transition makes the problem harder: the pupil must learn subject
content in English while still developing English proficiency.

## 6. Teacher conditions

No current national teacher-ratio or qualification figure has been extracted.
The World Bank document reports a shortage of qualified teachers and large class
sizes, but this profile does not convert that into a national ratio. Teacher
English proficiency and in-service support remain unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

The evidence supports designing for large classes and a language transition, not
for a well-resourced English-medium laboratory. No current national class-size
distribution has been reviewed.

## 8. Textbook availability

No current national pupil-textbook ratio has been verified. The lesson must not
require a textbook in every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

On mainland Tanzania, Kiswahili is the language of instruction in primary
education and English becomes the language of instruction in secondary education.
Zanzibar has separate arrangements. The exact transition support, the language of
examinations and classroom practice in a given school have not been reviewed.

The 2025 NECTA Form Two Learning Evaluation reports low mastery of English, the
primary language of instruction, affecting both teaching and learning. That is a
direct warning against assuming that a secondary lesson's English text is
accessible.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Kiswahili and English are the main school languages. Many pupils also speak a
community language. The pupil may understand a concept in Kiswahili but be
required to answer in English; the product must keep both visible.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. The lesson
must work offline and on paper.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power or a projector.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

The Standard 2 arithmetic benchmark was met by 17.5% of boys and 16.7% of girls,
a small gap in that measure. That does not establish gender equality in access,
retention or secondary completion. Disability, language, rural and income
barriers remain unverified.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

No current rural/urban comparison has been reviewed. The national Standard 2
figures do not describe a particular district or school.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

The 3Rs reform and structured foundational teaching are relevant. Research on
Tanzania's 3Rs curriculum reports improvements, but this profile has not verified
the effect size or its applicability to secondary OpenMind lessons. No intervention
is called proven for this product.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Support the Kiswahili-to-English transition explicitly.** A pupil may need
   the explanation in Kiswahili and the examination term in English.
2. **Do not equate English reading with subject understanding.**
3. **Place by prerequisite**, especially for foundational reading and arithmetic.
4. **Use teacher-led no-device and limited-materials modes.**
5. **Separate mainland and Zanzibar routes**; do not present one as the other.
6. **Do not claim CSEE alignment.**
