# Pakistan (PK)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Pakistan cannot be represented by one
> provincial or one medium-of-instruction route. This profile records national and
> provincial access figures; it does not establish a reviewed matriculation
> mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Pakistan · Secondary**, Classes 6–10, with a matriculation
route and a federal/Cambridge option. The country's provincial systems,
examination boards, madrasah streams and technical routes are not fully modelled.
A single national pathway would be misleading.

## 2. Curriculum

The provincial and federal syllabi, the mathematics/science sequence and the
current curriculum revision have not been reviewed. The registry's mapping
remains `mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

Matriculation and federal/Cambridge O/A Levels are recorded. Provincial board
formats, practical requirements and assessment rules have not been reviewed.

province with the highest exclusion.

## 5. Foundational skills

The ASER 2025 results show very low Grade 3 reading and division. A Class 6 or
Class 9 learner may still need foundational work. The product must not treat the
class label as evidence of mastery.

## 6. Teacher conditions

No current national teacher-ratio or qualification figure has been extracted.
Provincial deployment, teacher subject knowledge and in-service support remain
unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

No current national class-size observation has been reviewed. The product must
support large classes, shared materials and no-device teaching.

## 8. Textbook availability

No current national pupil-textbook ratio has been verified. The lesson must not
require a textbook in every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

Urdu and English are the main languages of schooling and examination, with
provincial languages used in some provision. The exact medium of instruction
varies by province, school type and subject; it has not been reviewed here.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Urdu, English, Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi and other provincial languages are
relevant. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for; it cannot be inferred
from the province or the school type.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. Household
mobile access is not school connectivity. The lesson must work offline and on
paper.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power or a projector.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

The 2023–24 data show more out-of-school girls than boys in Sindh and Khyber
Pakhtunkhwa, and Balochistan has the highest proportional exclusion. Gender
barriers interact with poverty, rurality and province; they are not captured by a
single national gap.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

Provincial and rural/urban differences are large. The national out-of-school rate
must not be presented as the typical village school, and a Punjab average must not
be presented as every Punjabi district.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Foundational catch-up, structured pedagogy, provincial curriculum support and
girls' access programmes are relevant. This pass did not review a Pakistani trial
of OpenMind or a specific intervention's effect size.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Make province and examination board explicit choices**; do not build one
   generic Pakistani pathway.
2. **Support Urdu/English and provincial-language explanation** with the
   examination term visible.
3. **Address girls' access barriers** by supporting shared devices, offline study
   and printed work, and by not requiring a personal account.
4. **Place by prerequisite**, not by Class 6–10 label.
5. **Do not claim matriculation alignment.**
6. **Do not use a Punjab or urban example as the national example.**
