# Rwanda (RW)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Rwanda is the clearest case of an
> official English-medium system where pupils' strongest language may still be
> Kinyarwanda. This profile does not establish a reviewed O/A Level mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Rwanda · Ordinary/Advanced Level**, S1–S6, with O Level at
the end of S3 and A Level at the end of S6, under NESA. Pre-primary, primary,
TVET and non-formal routes are not fully modelled.

## 2. Curriculum

The competence-based curriculum and the S1–S6 mathematics/science syllabi have
not been reviewed. The registry's mapping remains `mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

O Level and A Level are recorded. Their formats, practical requirements and
school-based assessment have not been reviewed.

## 5. Foundational skills

P3 pupils perform much better in Kinyarwanda than in English, while English is the
official medium across all levels. That is the central design tension: the
language of instruction is not necessarily the language in which the concept is
best understood.

## 6. Teacher conditions

The ESSP reports that only 4% of primary teachers met minimum English proficiency
standards (World Bank 2018) and that 35% of primary teachers lack formal teacher
training. The figures are older or administrative, but they support a teacher
language-support requirement. Current teacher subject knowledge remains
unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

No current national class-size observation has been reviewed. The product must
support large classes, shared materials and no-device teaching.

## 8. Textbook availability

No current national pupil-textbook ratio has been verified. The lesson must not
require a textbook in every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

English is the official medium of instruction across all levels, while
Kinyarwanda is the national language and the strongest language of many pupils.
The exact classroom practice, code-switching and assessment language rules have
not been reviewed.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Kinyarwanda and English are the central school languages; French and other
languages may also be present. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. The lesson
must work offline and on paper.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power or a projector.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

No current gender-disaggregated learning figure has been extracted. Disability,
language, rural and poverty barriers remain unverified.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

The 2021 assessment showed large provincial differences in English achievement
(32% in Kigali versus 4% in Eastern Province and 7% in Northern Province,
according to UNICEF's 2024 summary). That is a reason to avoid a single national
classroom assumption.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Teacher English-language support, bilingual explanation, structured pedagogy and
foundational catch-up are relevant. This pass did not review a Rwandan trial of
OpenMind or a specific intervention's effect size.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Explain in Kinyarwanda where needed while keeping the English examination
   term visible.**
2. **Support teachers' English proficiency**, not only pupils' language choice.
3. **Place by prerequisite**, especially for academic English and mathematics.
4. **Use teacher-led no-device and limited-materials modes.**
5. **Do not claim O/A Level alignment.**
6. **Do not infer a pupil's strongest language from the official medium.**
