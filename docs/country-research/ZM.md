# Zambia (ZM)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Zambia is a case for grouping by
> demonstrated level, not official grade. This profile records a very high
> learning-poverty estimate and a catch-up programme; it does not establish a
> reviewed ECZ mapping.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Zambia · Secondary**, Grades 8–12, ending in the School
Certificate under the Examinations Council of Zambia (ECZ), with a Junior
Secondary Certificate. Primary, early childhood, technical and non-formal routes
are not fully modelled.

## 2. Curriculum

The primary and secondary mathematics/science syllabi and the current curriculum
framework have not been reviewed. The registry's mapping remains `mapped`.

## 3. Examinations

The Junior Secondary Certificate and School Certificate are recorded. Their
formats, practical requirements and school-based assessment have not been reviewed.

The 98.5% estimate is so high that it should be read as "almost all children are
not achieving the reading benchmark in the underlying data", not as a precise
prediction for an individual child.

## 5. Foundational skills

The learning-poverty estimate and the existence of catch-up programmes point to
the same conclusion: a Grade 8 learner may be working at a much earlier level.
Grouping must be flexible and based on demonstrated need.

## 6. Teacher conditions

teaching time as challenges; this profile does not convert those into a national

## 9. Language policy

## 10. Common teaching languages

Kaonde and Luvale are relevant. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Zambia has implemented Catch-Up programmes using Teaching at the Right Level
approaches in public primary schools. That is a relevant model for flexible
grouping by demonstrated level. This pass did not review a Zambian effect
evaluation of OpenMind and makes no effect-size claim.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Group by demonstrated level, not grade**, and allow movement between groups.
2. **Do not permanently label a learner** as "foundational" or "weak".
3. **Use teacher-led no-device and limited-materials modes.**
4. **Place by prerequisite** before Grade 8 content.
5. **Do not claim ECZ alignment.**
6. **Keep the pathway short and repeatable** for pupils who need more practice.
