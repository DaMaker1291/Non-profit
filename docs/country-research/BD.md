# Bangladesh (BD)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** This is a sourced first profile, not a
> reviewed curriculum mapping. It establishes design constraints and hypotheses.
> It does not establish that OpenMind's Class 6–10 concepts match the Bangladeshi
> syllabus, and no Bangladeshi teacher or subject specialist has reviewed the
> lessons.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records one national pathway, **Bangladesh · Secondary**, covering
Classes 6–10 and ending in the SSC. The register also records the
Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education and the JSC/SSC examinations.

The primary system (Classes 1–5) is not the registry's secondary route, but it
matters for placement: a Class 8 learner may still need foundational reading or
numeracy. The primary curriculum was revised in 2022, and the next National
Student Assessment is scheduled for 2026. Neither the primary nor secondary
syllabus has been mapped concept by concept in this pass.

**Do not infer:** that Classes 6–10 are the only secondary route, that every
school follows the same board, or that a learner in Class 8 has mastered the
primary curriculum.

## 2. Curriculum

The National Student Assessment (NSA) assesses Bangla literacy and numeracy in
Grades 3 and 5. Its 2022 framework added pre-reading and early-numeracy items
from earlier grades to detect pandemic-related gaps. That is evidence that
foundational skills are a live curriculum concern, not evidence that OpenMind's
secondary lessons are aligned.

The secondary SSC syllabus, subject sequence, practical requirements and
revision date have **not** been reviewed. The registry's source is a structural
record; it is not a subject-level curriculum source.

## 3. Examinations

The registry records JSC and SSC under the Board of Intermediate and Secondary
Education. The assessment formats, paper structure, practical examinations and
grading rules have not been reviewed. NSA is a national sample assessment, not an
individual diagnostic and not a school-leaving examination.

Primary teachers are generalists; the same source describes a one-year
pre-service Diploma in Primary Education requirement for state-school teachers.
That does not establish secondary teacher subject knowledge or current in-service
support.

## 7. Classroom conditions

The evidence supports a design assumption of **teacher-led, large-class teaching
with limited individual attention**, not a one-device-per-pupil classroom. State
primary classes of 40–70 are reported; the national pupil-teacher ratio is much
lower than that range, which is exactly why a national ratio cannot be used as a
class-size proxy.

The evidence does **not** establish the distribution of class sizes, the prevalence
of multigrade or double-shift schooling, or how much individual feedback a pupil
receives. Those remain unverified.

## 8. Textbook availability

The 2024 study reports that state-produced English textbooks are distributed free
to all students in January. That is not a general textbook-availability measure
for every subject or every school type. No current secondary pupil-textbook ratio
has been collected here.

## 9. Language policy

Bangla is the main language of instruction in government primary schools. The
World Bank's 2020 review records 41 languages in Bangladesh, 16 in common use,
and reports that indigenous students outside the main Bengali ethnic group are
half as likely to complete primary school; one reason given is that they do not
understand the national language used in school.

The exact current policy for indigenous-language education, the treatment of
Rohingya learners, and language practice in madrasahs and English-medium schools
have not been reviewed.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Bangla is the dominant teaching language; English is a compulsory subject and is
used in English-medium provision. Other languages are spoken in homes and in
some local-language education programmes.

**Do not infer a pupil's strongest language from the national language.** A
Bangladeshi classroom may contain pupils whose home language is not Bangla.

## 13. Internet

The administrative figure above is an institution-level measure. It does not
establish that a teacher can stream video, that a class set of devices can
connect, or that the connection works during a lesson. OpenMind's offline and
print paths remain necessary.

## 14. Devices

No current, nationally representative pupil-device ownership figure has been
collected for Bangladesh. A school computer for pedagogical purposes is not a
pupil device, and a household phone is not a personal learning device.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

NSA 2022 found no overall achievement gap between boys and girls, which is a
notable result but not a claim that gender inequality is absent. Out-of-school
rates and completion differ by level; disability, indigenous-language, refugee
and rural exclusion are not resolved by a national gender parity result.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

Most out-of-school children live in rural areas in the 2020 UNICEF fact sheets.
The current profile has not established how class size, teacher deployment,
connectivity, textbook delivery or language use differ between rural and urban
schools. A national average must not be presented as the typical rural school.

## 17. Offline requirements

The country's administrative connectivity figures are improving, but the design
requirement is not "most schools are online". It is:

- the lesson must be usable when the teacher has one computer and no pupil devices;
- the pupil worksheet and separate teacher key must be printable;
- the assessment must be usable on paper;
- the teacher must not need a permanent connection during the lesson;
- a downloaded or printed pack must remain usable when the connection fails.

## 18. Cost constraints

Primary textbooks are reported as free, but private tutoring and out-of-pocket
spending are used to compensate for poor-quality schooling. OpenMind must not add
hidden costs: no required personal device, no mandatory data bundle, no
print-only design that assumes every family can print at home.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

The NSA evidence supports **foundational-skill assessment and targeted teaching**
before grade-level content. Structured pedagogy, explicit instruction and
printable teacher guidance are plausible responses, but this pass did not review
a Bangladeshi trial of OpenMind or a specific structured-pedagogy programme.
No effect size is claimed.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Bengali-first is a content requirement, not an interface toggle.** The
   current interface translation does not establish that a lesson's explanation,
   examples and terminology have been reviewed in Bangla.
2. **Place by demonstrated prerequisites.** A Class 8 learner who cannot handle
   fractions must be able to work on fractions without being told they are "slow".
3. **Teacher-led no-device mode is the primary delivery mode**, with printed
   worksheet, separate key and board-based group work.
4. **Large-class routines must be visible in the lesson run of show**, not left
   to a teacher to invent.
5. **Do not assume a science laboratory or projector.** Practical alternatives
   must be honest about what was and was not used.
6. **Do not infer home language from the national language.**
7. **Do not claim curriculum alignment.** The SSC mapping remains unreviewed.

## 21. Unverified questions

- What is the current Class 6–10 mathematics and science syllabus, and when was
  each subject last revised?
- Which board's assessment format applies in a given school, and what practical
  work is required?
- What is the actual distribution of class sizes, and how common are multigrade
  and double-shift classes?
- How reliable is school electricity and internet during the school day?
- Which local-language education programmes operate, where, and with what
  teaching materials?
- What is the current secondary textbook ratio, and are textbooks shared?
- How are learners with disabilities included, and what assistive resources exist?
- What happens to learners who are over-age, returning after interruption, or in
  non-formal education?
- Which examples, units and terminology in OpenMind's current lessons are
  unfamiliar or wrong for Bangladeshi classrooms?
