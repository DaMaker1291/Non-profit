# Nepal (NP)

> **Research status: `INITIAL_RESEARCH`.** Nepal's linguistic diversity is the
> central design fact in this first profile. The profile does not establish a
> reviewed SEE/NEB mapping or a reviewed Nepali translation.

## 1. National school structure

The registry records **Nepal · Secondary**, Grades 8–12, with SEE at the end of
Grade 10 and NEB Grade 12, under the National Examinations Board. Basic education,
secondary phases and local-government variation are not fully modelled by the
registry.

## 2. Curriculum

The national curriculum and the Grade 8–12 mathematics/science syllabi have not
been reviewed. The registry's concept mapping remains `mapped`; no claim of
curriculum alignment is made.

## 3. Examinations

SEE and NEB Grade 12 are recorded. Their paper formats, practical requirements,
internal assessment and provincial/local variation have not been reviewed.

A widely quoted 79% Grade 5 mathematics below-proficiency figure was not used here
because the report's proficiency table was not extracted in this pass. The
verified summary is that fewer than 40% reached the required proficiency across
the assessed subjects. That is enough to reject a "grade level equals mastery"
assumption without inventing a subject-specific number.

## 5. Foundational skills

The NASA result and the language evidence point to foundational learning and
comprehension as central. A learner may be in Grade 8 while still developing
Nepali or English academic language.

## 6. Teacher conditions

No current national teacher-ratio, qualification or deployment figure has been
extracted. Teacher language proficiency and training remain unverified.

## 7. Classroom conditions

No current national class-size observation has been reviewed. The product must
support large, multigrade and low-resource classes rather than assume a device per
pupil or a well-equipped laboratory.

## 8. Textbook availability

No current national pupil-textbook ratio has been verified. The lesson must not
require a textbook in every pupil's hands.

## 9. Language policy

The Constitution recognizes all mother tongues as national languages and
guarantees the right to education in one's mother tongue within the legal
framework. The National Education Policy 2076 supports mother-tongue-based
education up to the basic level and a trilingual teaching policy. Implementation
varies by local government, school and community.

This profile has not verified the language policy of a particular local
government or the language actually used in a particular classroom.

## 10. Common teaching languages

Nepali is the most widely spoken language and a major school language; English is
also taught and used in some provision. Maithili and many other languages are
spoken in homes. A pupil's strongest language must be asked for.

## 11. Technology access

No current school-level internet or computer figure has been verified. Household
mobile access is not school connectivity. The lesson must work offline and on
paper.

## 12. Electricity

No current school electricity figure has been verified. The lesson must not
require mains power or a projector; a charged teacher device and printed pack are
the baseline.

## 13. Internet

No current school internet figure has been verified. The lesson must work after a
download or print, without a live request during class.

## 14. Devices

No current pupil-device ownership figure has been collected. Shared-device and
no-device modes are required; a personal account is not.

## 15. Gender and inclusion

No current gender-disaggregated learning figure has been extracted. Caste,
ethnicity, language, disability, geography and poverty barriers remain unverified
and must not be reduced to a national average.

## 16. Rural and urban differences

NASA's sample covers 75 districts, but this profile has not reviewed its
province/geography breakdown. Remote mountain and rural schools may face
constraints that a national estimate cannot show.

## 17. Offline requirements

The lesson must be printable and locally usable; the teacher must not need a live
connection, a projector or pupil devices. Assessment must work on paper and be
entered later.

## 18. Cost constraints

The essential path must not require a personal device, data purchase, home
printing or paid subscription. School printing and shared devices must be viable.

## 19. Relevant educational interventions

Mother-tongue-based multilingual education, structured pedagogy and foundational
catch-up are relevant. This pass did not review a Nepali trial of OpenMind or a
specific intervention's effect size.

## 20. OpenMind product implications

1. **Do not provide only a Nepali translation.** A pupil may need a local language
   explanation while learning Nepali/English subject terminology.
2. **Support bilingual explanation** with a consistent glossary and the
   examination term visible.
3. **Place by prerequisite**, especially for academic language and mathematics.
4. **Use teacher-led no-device and limited-materials modes.**
5. **Do not claim SEE/NEB alignment.**
6. **Do not infer language from ethnicity or geography.**
