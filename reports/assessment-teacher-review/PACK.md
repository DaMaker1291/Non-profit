**Named misconceptions**

- `absorption-is-eating` — Absorption is the same as eating. Why wrong: Eating puts food into the alimentary canal; absorption moves digested molecules across the gut wall into the blood. A learner can eat enough and still absorb too little if the absorbing surface is damaged.
- `villi-are-muscles` — Villi push food through the gut. Why wrong: Villi are finger-like folds of the gut lining. They increase surface area and contain blood capillaries and a lacteal; they do not provide the muscular contraction that moves gut contents. Peristalsis does that.
- `bile-digests-fat` — Bile is an enzyme that digests fat. Why wrong: Bile is not an enzyme. It emulsifies fat into smaller droplets, increasing the surface area on which lipase can act. The chemical digestion is carried out by lipase.
- `energy-label-is-body-energy` — The packet's energy value is the energy the body gets from the food. Why wrong: A food label's energy value is measured by complete combustion in a calorimeter. A school burning experiment loses heat to the surroundings, may burn the food incompletely and heats the apparatus as well as the water. The two values answer related but not identical questions.
- `surface-area-alone` — More surface area is always enough. Why wrong: Surface area increases the opportunity for exchange, but absorption also requires a thin barrier, a concentration gradient and a transport route. A larger surface with no blood supply or a thick barrier is not automatically more effective.
- `egestion-is-excretion` — Egestion and excretion are the same process. Why wrong: Egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal. Excretion removes metabolic waste produced by cells, such as carbon dioxide and urea. The substances and their origins are different.
- `fat-products-are-sugars` — Fat is digested into glucose. Why wrong: Lipase hydrolyses a fat into fatty acids and glycerol. Glucose is a product of carbohydrate digestion, not fat digestion, and the body cannot obtain it by breaking a fat with lipase.

**Reviewer notes**:

---

**Named misconceptions**

- `power-is-energy` — Power and energy are the same quantity. Why wrong: Energy is the amount transferred; power is the rate of transfer. A 100 W lamp left on for 10 s transfers 1000 J; a 10 W lamp left on for 100 s transfers the same energy at a tenth of the power. The units differ for the same reason: joules measure an amount, watts measure joules per second.
- `efficiency-is-energy-loss` — Efficiency means energy has been lost. Why wrong: Energy is conserved. An inefficient device does not destroy energy; it transfers a smaller fraction of its input usefully and the rest to the surroundings, usually by heating. Efficiency is the ratio of useful output to total input, not the amount that disappeared.
- `specific-heat-is-total` — Specific heat capacity is the total energy needed to heat an object. Why wrong: Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C. The total energy for an object also depends on its mass and on the temperature change, which is why Q = m c ΔT has three factors and not one.
- `temperature-is-energy` — A higher temperature always means more thermal energy. Why wrong: Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of particles; thermal energy also depends on how many particles there are and on the substance's specific heat capacity. A small cup of water at 90 °C can contain less thermal energy than a bath at 40 °C.

#### `mt-01` — 4 marks — table

Concept: `linear-equations` · archetype: `table-completion` · item review: **unreviewed**

**Prompt**: Complete the table of values for y = 2x + 1.

**Model answer**: At x = −1, y = −1; at x = 0, y = 1; at x = 1, y = 3; at x = 2, y = 5.

**Marking criteria**:

- `mt-01-c1` (answer, 1 mark): x = −1 gives y = −1
- `mt-01-c2` (answer, 1 mark): x = 0 gives y = 1
- `mt-01-c3` (answer, 1 mark): x = 1 gives y = 3
- `mt-01-c4` (answer, 1 mark): x = 2 gives y = 5
- Partial credit: Each blank cell is marked independently, so two correct cells earn two marks. The rule is the same for every cell: multiply x by 2, then add 1.

#### `mt-03` — 3 marks — extended-response

Concept: `quadratics` · archetype: `explain-square-sign` · item review: **unreviewed**

**Prompt**: A learner substitutes x = −1 into y = x² − 2 and says the answer is 3, because (−1)² = 1 and 1 + 2 = 3. Explain the mistake and give the correct value of y.

**Model answer**: (−1)² means (−1) × (−1), which is 1, not −1. The rule subtracts 2 after squaring, so y = 1 − 2 = −1. The learner changed the subtraction into an addition.

**Marking criteria**:

- `mt-03-c1` (evidence, 1 mark): squares −1 correctly and gets 1
- `mt-03-c2` (evidence, 1 mark): keeps the subtraction outside the square: 1 − 2, not 1 + 2
  - Diagnostic belief: `square-negative-is-negative`
- `mt-03-c3` (evidence, 1 mark): states the corrected value y = −1
- Partial credit: Each idea is a separate mark: the square, the operation after it, and the corrected value. A correct value with no reason earns at most the value mark.

**Reviewer decision**: ☐ accurate  ☐ changes needed  ☐ reject

**Named misconceptions**

- `square-negative-is-negative` — Squaring a negative number makes it negative. Why wrong: (−1)² means (−1) × (−1), and a negative times a negative is positive. The square is 1, not −1. A minus sign outside the square is a separate subtraction applied after squaring, so x² − 2 at x = −1 is 1 − 2 = −1.
